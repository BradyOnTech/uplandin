# German Shorthaired Pointer reference research

Authoritative references and modeling translation for a low-poly Three.js dog. The current (effective January 1, 2023) American standard is the primary target because the game is US upland hunting; the FCI standard is useful corroboration and for the natural-tail option.

## Conformation and distinctive silhouette

- The GSP is a medium, athletic, symmetrical dog built for power, endurance, speed, and agility—not an ultralight English Pointer and not a heavy retriever. The essential silhouette is a **short, strong back over a ground-covering frame**, with a clean head, laid-back shoulders, deep chest, apparent tuck-up, broad hips, and muscular quarters. ([GSPCA current breed standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [GSPCA breed-type guide](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/GSPCA-JE-GSP-Breed-Type23.pdf))
- Body length, measured forechest to rear projection of rump, is square or slightly longer than height at the withers. GSPCA educational material quantifies “slightly” as commonly about 5% and no more than 10%; for this model, target **body-length : withers-height = 1.03–1.06**. Males are 23–25 in at the withers and 55–70 lb; females 21–23 in and 45–60 lb. ([GSPCA standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [AKC Gazette, July 2025, pp. 36–37](https://images.akc.org/pdf/gazette/july-2025.pdf))
- The topline is firm and subtly slopes from defined withers toward the tail; the loin is short, broad, muscular, and straight or only slightly arched. The chest is deeper than broad and reaches the elbow, while the underline rises into a clear tuck-up. In side view this produces a **wedge, not a tube**. ([FCI Standard No. 119, pp. 3–4](https://www.fci.be/Nomenclature/Standards/119g07-en.pdf); [GSPCA desired characteristics](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/GSPCA-JE-GSP-Desired-Characteristics23.pdf))
- Shoulders are well laid back (the US material describes near 45°), with a long upper arm and elbow close to the body. Rear angulation should balance the front: long muscular thighs, bent stifle, short/strong well-angulated hock, and compact round-to-spoon feet. This is what lets the dog look planted and powerful without becoming bulky. ([GSPCA “short back” guide](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Short-back-Standing-over-plenty-ground.pdf); [current GSPCA illustrated standard, printed pp. 8–9](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/GSP-Illustrated-Standard-January-2023.pdf))
- The head must read differently from an English Pointer: reasonably broad and gently rounded skull, modest/gradual stop created partly by the eyebrow, long deep muzzle approximately equal to skull length, strong jaw, and a large slightly projecting nose. Eyes are medium, almond-shaped, and dark brown. Ears are broad, high-set just above eye level, flat against the head, bluntly rounded, and reach approximately the corner of the mouth when drawn forward. ([GSPCA standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [FCI standard, pp. 2–3](https://www.fci.be/Nomenclature/Standards/119g07-en.pdf))
- The tail is high-set and strong at the base. The AKC standard calls for about **40% of natural length** after docking and horizontal carriage in motion. The FCI standard describes about half-length when docked and explicitly permits a natural tail where docking is prohibited; a natural tail reaches the hock and is straight or slightly sabre-shaped. For a recognizable US field-dog silhouette, the 40% tail is the clearest default, but this should remain a model option. ([GSPCA standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [FCI standard, p. 4](https://www.fci.be/Nomenclature/Standards/119g07-en.pdf))

### Low-poly modeling priorities

1. Start from the Setter rig proportions, but remove all feathering and lengthen the visible leg/ground clearance slightly.
2. Make the torso shorter, firmer, deeper through the sternum, and more tucked behind the ribs; keep muscle planes lean rather than rounded.
3. Broaden the skull and muzzle relative to the Setter, reduce the stop, enlarge the nose, and make the ears shorter, broader, and close-hanging.
4. Emphasize a laid-back scapula, long upper arm, powerful rear thigh, and compact paw; avoid thin stilts or a barrel chest.
5. Use a taut, close silhouette with no fur cards. A few deliberate planes at shoulder, ribcage, loin, and thigh will sell the short coat better than surface noise.

## Coat, color, and markings

- Hair is short, dense/thick, close, and tough or hard to the touch; it is softer, thinner, and shorter on the head and ears. The coat should look smooth and taut, but not glossy-plastic or velvety. ([GSPCA standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [FCI standard, p. 5](https://www.fci.be/Nomenclature/Standards/119g07-en.pdf))
- Under the current US standard, valid families are **solid liver; liver-and-white ticked, patched-and-ticked, or liver roan; solid black; and equivalent black-and-white ticked, patched-and-ticked, or black roan**. No pattern or head marking is preferred. Red, orange, lemon, tan, and solid white are disqualifying in the US standard. ([GSPCA current standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [GSPCA coat-pattern guide](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/GSPCA-JE-GSP-Coat-Patterns.pdf))
- The older 2015 illustrated standard still says black is disqualifying because it reproduces the 1992 wording. That color rule is obsolete: the 2023 revision explicitly added black. Use the current GSPCA web standard for palette legality, while retaining the illustrated PDF for anatomy and movement. ([AKC notice of 2023 revision](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/12-2022-Gazette.pdf); [GSPCA current standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/))
- A strong first game skin would be a solid liver head and ears, white/liver-roan torso, one or two asymmetrical liver patches, and lighter ticking on lower legs. Render ticking as small irregular flecks or a low-resolution procedural mask, not large Dalmatian-like circles. Roan should read as a dense intermixture at gameplay distance.

## Working gait and hunting behavior

- The conformation gait is explicitly smooth, lithe, efficient, and ground-covering: forelegs reach ahead without high elbow lift or hackney action; hindquarters provide strong drive; feet stay close to the ground; the topline remains firm without rolling, pitching, or sagging; and faster movement converges toward single tracking. ([GSPCA standard](https://gspca.org/breed/standard/); [current GSPCA illustrated standard, printed pp. 18–20](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/GSP-Illustrated-Standard-January-2023.pdf); [GSPCA breed-type guide](https://gspca.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/GSPCA-JE-GSP-Breed-Type23.pdf))
- Official field-trial rules value desire, boldness, independence, a **fast but attractive** run, intelligent searching of likely objectives, useful quartering, use of wind/terrain, and periodic check-ins with the handler. ([AKC Pointing Breed Field Trial Procedure, pp. 41–44](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/AKC1978_RFTPNT_0325_WEB.pdf))
- The official sources do not prescribe one breed-specific field cadence. The safest game interpretation is therefore an inference: give the GSP a fast, economical lope/canter for routine searching, brief gallop surges when relocating or committing to scent, and a lower-amplitude vertical bounce than the Setter. The dog should cover ground with long reach and rear drive, not churn its legs.
- Search “wiggle” should come primarily from **course changes and sensory behavior**, not a rolling spine: broad quartering arcs, a small lateral chest/loin flex into turns, counter-rotating pelvis, horizontal tail used as a rudder, independent nose/head probes into the wind, then a quick body alignment behind the head when scent strengthens. Keep the topline visually stable, because the parent-club movement material explicitly rejects rolling and pitching.
- Suggested animation character relative to the English Setter: slightly quicker acceleration, longer ground-covering stride, stronger hind push, tighter/shorter coat inertia, less ear and tail lag, and more decisive direction changes. It should still be more economical and versatile than the English Pointer’s sustained all-out sprint character.

## Recommended build target

For the first distinct GSP rather than a recolored Setter, lock these recognition anchors: **short dense coat with no feathering; square/slightly long deep-and-tucked torso; broad gradual-stop head and projecting nose; broad close-hanging ears; strong rear quarters; compact feet; horizontal 40%-length tail; liver-roan or patched-and-ticked coloration; and low-waste, ground-covering motion**.
