# Quail Fields validation baseline

Ordinary gameplay and staged asset reviews are different evidence. A valid hunt state must be recorded even when its rendering has a defect. Passing a capture script does not establish production quality.

**Confirmed original failure**

On 2026-09-04 the unmodified command `node tools3d/capture.mjs --shots gameplay-point --breed gsp --area quail-fields --out /tmp/uplandin-capture-baseline` failed after 30,000 simulation ticks. Its output reported `still=true`, `planted=false` and supporting paw gaps of `0`, `0.1005`, and `0.1006` metres. The error said the dog never reached point even though it had.

Two independent browser runs probing every tick both reached an authoritative GSP point at tick 34, at world position `(6.0105, 0.4392)`. The render audit correctly identified the same GSP and position. The point predicate rejected it because the hind paws floated roughly ten centimetres above the ground. Static poses moved the entire dog down until its lowest supporting foot touched; this could not independently plant its rear legs.

The old screenshot also used FOV 40 instead of the normal FOV 70. Capture mode used a different spawn, movement scale, work anchor and dog range. Its point image was therefore unsuitable as evidence of ordinary play even if the contact defect were corrected.

**Corrections to the evidence pipeline**

`Hunt3DSystem` now uses the same initial heel, cast-off, range, anchor and movement scale in capture and normal modes. Capture controls the clock only. A regression replays 900 identical camera-input ticks through the live and capture adapters from each of the actual Quail Fields drops, comparing dog position, state, scent stage, covey flush distance/count and corresponding bird outcomes. The regression failed on the original spawn mismatch before the adapter fix and passes afterward. A second regression confirms that capture holds the dog during an airborne rise exactly as the normal fixed update does; it failed before both paths shared the same advance method.

The point capture predicate now observes a stable authoritative point independently of paw clearance. Contact defects remain visible in audit metadata and warnings; `--require-contact` makes them a separate failure. The replacement GSP rig must establish its own contact proof before that issue can be considered resolved.

`tools3d/capture.mjs` defaults to `gameplay-arrival,gameplay-walk,gameplay-point`. These launch ordinary play, use the normal camera and real browser keyboard/mouse input, and include the gameplay interface. `--quality high|lite`, `--width`, and `--height` are explicit. Default output is 1920 × 1080 at DPR 1. The requested tier and actual telemetry are recorded beside each image.

Historical named compositions remain available as staged asset reviews. Their JSON manifests say `staged-asset-review`, record their changed lens and camera, and explicitly state that they are not normal gameplay evidence. The former enlarged-lens point is named `review-point-long-lens`; staged gun frames are `review-gun-carry` and `review-gun-mount`.

**Continuous hunt evidence**

`node tools3d/playthrough.mjs --drop south-gate --quality high --out /tmp/quail-south` records a normal browser hunt. Repeat with `--drop west-track`. It uses ordinary input for walking, mounting, shooting, reloading and selecting finish/replay. Read-only dog state guides the walk-in; read-only airborne bird positions guide mouse aiming. It never calls `setPose`, `stepSim`, `triggerFlush` or `downBird`.

The output includes a silent, continuous MP4, screenshot checkpoints, a timestamped NDJSON trace and a JSON manifest with browser/host details, actual rendering telemetry, errors and acceptance results. The loop requires point, natural proximity flush, deliberate miss, an aimed real shot, searching for a fall, carrying, delivery, finish summary and replay. A failure preserves its last state and image. An automated aiming success is functional evidence, not proof that aiming feels good to a human.

Use `--until arrival|walk|point` for shorter diagnostics and `--no-video` for measurements without video-encoding overhead. A recorded video's 30 fps encoding rate is not measured game performance. Record the hardware, browser, backing resolution, quality, frame-time distribution and active environment when claiming a 60 fps desktop baseline. Desktop emulation or the lite quality tier does not establish physical phone performance, thermal stability or touch usability.

**Outstanding validation**

The original visual baseline predates the production slice changes. The ordinary browser loop must be rerun against the final dog, world and controls. The editable GSP rig, foot contact across slopes, continuous gameplay appearance, rendering performance, repeated hunts, actual career persistence and real-device mobile behavior remain separate acceptance requirements. A blank or incomplete manifest is not a pass.

**Evidence produced during implementation**

The fresh ordinary-input west-track run with the Blender GSP completed point, proximity rise, deliberate miss, real hit, search, carry, delivery, summary and replay with no browser errors. Its trace is `/tmp/quail-rigged-west/west-track-high.ndjson`; the matching manifest records Chrome 148, ANGLE Metal on Apple M1 Pro, 1920 × 1080 high quality and its actual application bundle. Twenty-three sampled point frames had maximum supporting paw clearance of 4.87 mm in that snapshot.

The subsequent south-gate lite run also completed the full sequence. `/tmp/quail-rigged-south-lite/south-gate-lite.mp4` is a silent continuous recording including the game interface; the matching JSON and NDJSON contain inputs, states, browser details and measured rendering resolution. Nine carried-state samples placed the rendered bird exactly at the measured mouth socket. The 1280 × 720 video duration was 47.1667 seconds against 47.163 seconds of captured timestamp duration. Only 341 distinct browser frames were available, so the 30 fps output must not be used to claim smooth 30 fps motion. Actual rendering frame times and recorded video cadence are different measurements. These initial runs also used uncapped Chromium flags, so their sub-refresh frame intervals are diagnostic throughput rather than default-browser performance. Ordinary gameplay tooling now keeps browser frame limiting and vsync defaults; `--uncapped` is an explicit diagnostic option recorded in the manifest.

The recorder now collects bounded JPEG frames through the browser protocol and encodes their timestamped durations after the hunt. This prevents PNG encoding from blocking input during fast events. Older Puppeteer PNG/VP9 recordings had slow encoding and stretched output timing; their wall-clock traces remain functional evidence, but their videos do not prove animation speed.

`tools3d/validate-interface.mjs` passed explicit-entry pause, hunter/dog freeze during pause, cleared movement after resume, injected WebGL context loss/recovery, and emulated-touch movement, look, mount and pause-reset checks. Its touch fire check confirms the existing rule that a shot outside an active rise does not consume a shell; that lifecycle test does not establish touch shooting during a rise. `/tmp/quail-interface-verified/interface.json` contains the results and limitations.

The earlier `tools3d/validate-offline.mjs` inspected the fresh installed production cache and confirmed the shell and all three GSP LOD files were physically present. Its report at `/tmp/quail-offline/offline.json` records 21 service-worker responses and cache `uplandin-v3-6e7391e150b5240d`. Its page-only offline emulation did not block worker-originated network fetches, so the subsequent drop, finish and replay sequence is insufficient evidence of a complete outage. Those offline availability claims are superseded by the stronger proxy-boundary test documented in `docs/3d/offline-preservation.md`; the physical cache inventory remains valid historical evidence.

These are named implementation snapshots, not a declaration that every later asset or code edit is verified. The final bundle must be reconciled against its own run manifests. Broad route coverage, sustained physical-device performance, audio quality and subjective animation/visual finish require their own evidence. The later career and active-rise touch checks below cover those additional functional paths.

**Default-browser contact smoke**

The subsequent headed Chromium run used default frame limiting and vsync, no recording, the Apple M1 Pro Metal renderer, and 1920 × 1080 high quality. Its roughly 22-second opening walk-to-point sequence recorded 2,580 frame intervals: median 8.3 ms, 95th percentile 8.4 ms, 99th percentile 8.5 ms. The host appears to refresh at 120 Hz. This narrow sequence is evidence of rendering headroom on this machine, not complete-route or mobile performance proof. `/tmp/quail-default-headed-contact/south-gate-high.json` includes the actual launch arguments and bundle identity.

The same smoke recorded eight pairs with equal touchdown `plantId`, more than 90% contact, both feet locked, the same moving clip and at most 250 ms between observations. Their maximum horizontal paw displacement was 17.36 cm, mean 4.31 cm, with nine contact-limit resets. This small sample flags a locomotion/contact concern for further remediation; it does not support a no-sliding claim. Endpoint-only measurements without matching touchdown identity are explicitly uncertified because a complete swing/replant may happen between probes. Later harness runs preserve the worst pair's clip, leg, touchdown id, positions and interval for diagnosis.

A boot token and a coarse image-variation guard now reject screenshots taken across an unexpected reload or with effectively uniform central output. The guard rejected the actual blank `rise-wide.png` produced during a development reload, accepted the normal gameplay frame, and rejected a mismatched boot token. These are failure checks, not a visual-quality score.

The corrected gait snapshot (`three-3p6geK79.js`) repeated the same headed, default-vsync opening sequence. Its runtime reported `clip=trot`, `performance=stalk`, `posture=stalk` at approximately 3 m/s. Across 106 certified same-plant support pairs, maximum horizontal displacement fell to 15.55 mm and mean displacement to 1.24 mm; six contact-limit resets remained. The worst pair is preserved in `/tmp/quail-default-headed-gait-fixed/south-gate-high.json`. Frame intervals remained 8.3/8.4/8.4 ms at median/95th/99th percentile. This replaces the prior fast-stalk gait result for that opening sequence, while broader turns, slopes and gaits still need their own checks.

Normal `--until point` captures now preserve the initial point event and wait 750 ms of ordinary play before a separately labeled settled-point screenshot. Unexpected state changes during that wait fail the capture; the simulation is never forced to hold.

**Career persistence and active-rise touch input**

`tools3d/validate-career.mjs` creates disposable browser contexts with a labeled GSP career fixture, then calls the ordinary-input hunt harness with `--play career` and `--play quick`. Only the initial profile is seeded. The hunt uses real movement and shots, and settlement must remain stable while the summary is visible, after replay, and after reload. No user browser or profile is opened. The standalone gameplay harness now records the career value at settlement, checks it again after a half-second of summary display, and preserves the completed tally.

The career run against `three-3p6geK79.js` completed one real hit and retrieval. The summary awarded the GSP 6 XP, the hunter 3 XP, and advanced the home-hunt calendar exactly one week. The persisted career recorded one hunt and the same awards; replay and reload did not settle it again. `/tmp/quail-career-persistence/career.json` and the career check within `/tmp/quail-career-persistence/career-persistence.json` preserve this result. That combined invocation subsequently failed to obtain pointer lock in its second headed browser context, so its overall report correctly remains failed and is not a Quick Hunt pass.

`tools3d/validate-touch-rise.mjs` passed on the same immutable bundle. At an emulated coarse-pointer viewport of 844 × 390, touch movement and look reached a natural point and walk-in covey rise. Touch Aim mounted the gun, Fire consumed exactly one shell while birds remained airborne, Reload restored capacity, and touch Pause froze play. `/tmp/quail-touch-active-rise/touch-rise.json` and its timestamped trace preserve the observations. This closes the active-rise routing gap in the earlier lifecycle test; it does not establish real phone ergonomics or performance.

A separate fresh-context Quick Hunt run completed the same real-input miss, hit, retrieval, summary and replay sequence. The seeded career remained byte-identical through the summary, replay and an additional reload. `/tmp/quail-quick-persistence/career-persistence.json` is the passing Quick Hunt isolation report.

The full career and Quick Hunt paths also provided broader contact samples than the opening walk. Career recorded 99 equal-plant support pairs with a 2.98 mm mean but an 18.53 cm maximum (lope, left foreleg, plant 37 over 91 ms), and 20 contact-limit resets. Quick Hunt recorded 55 pairs with a 4.00 mm mean and a 16.21 cm maximum (trot, right hindleg, plant 37 over 83 ms), and 104 resets. That runtime could relocate an anchor when its correction exceeded 16 cm while retaining the same stance identity. These historical outliers led to the subsequent fixes and evidence in [dog contact corrections](dog-contact-corrections.md); they do not describe the later corrected checkpoint. The corresponding full traces and worst-pair records remain preserved in the career and Quick Hunt manifests above. Rendered bird position matched the measured mouth socket exactly in 77 career and 84 Quick Hunt carried-state samples.

The combined headed career/Quick Hunt rerun passed after the harness explicitly brought each new page to the front before play. `/tmp/quail-career-persistence-focused/career-persistence.json` records both successful isolation checks in one invocation. The earlier pointer-lock failure is retained as harness evidence, while this run verifies the correction.

`tools3d/validate-graphics-fallback.mjs` launches Chromium with WebGL actually disabled and checks startup recovery, a hidden loader, absent game readiness, visible retry and main-menu controls, and retry navigation. The [durable report](dog-contact-evidence/graphics-fallback.json) verifies this recovery on `boot3d-jniLcY8b.js`, including a successful retry back to the same recovery screen with no uncaught errors. Expected WebGL initialization errors are recorded separately.

**Revised anatomy checkpoint**

The later immutable `boot3d-BHq7gkFx.js` build includes the corrected adult GSP walking sequence and the offline menu fallback. Ordinary complete hunts pass from [West Track High](anatomy-gameplay-evidence/west-track-high.json), [South Gate Lite](anatomy-gameplay-evidence/anatomy-south-gate-lite.json), and [South Gate High](anatomy-gameplay-evidence/anatomy-south-gate-high.json). Its brief close delivery also does not prove a sustained carrying performance.

A separate [desktop performance summary](anatomy-gameplay-evidence/desktop-performance-summary.json) records a complete default-headed-browser High hunt on Apple M1 Pro, Chrome 148.0.7778.97, at 1920×1080 and DPR 1 with no video capture. The final rolling 3,600 frame intervals have median 8.3 ms and 95th/99th percentiles of 8.4 ms; 4,910 frames were observed in total, and the maximum was 175 ms. The percentiles describe the rolling window, not every frame of the hunt. This is useful desktop evidence with a remaining outlier, not sustained thermal or physical mobile proof.

The [stronger offline preservation report](offline-preservation-evidence/report.json) passes ten checks on its named earlier `boot3d-8t4TfEap.js` snapshot. It blocks worker-originated network traffic at a local proxy boundary and verifies a cold menu without cached artwork, actual UI relaunch of cached Quail 3D, and restoration of real menu art after reconnecting. It also verifies online 2D movement and summary preservation in a separate context. Later presentation edits require their own relevant checks and must not inherit an unspecified final-build certification.
